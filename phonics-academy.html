Sounds and spelling patterns

This course covers common phoneme–grapheme correspondences, including irregular and dialect-dependent patterns. The same spelling can represent different sounds in different words. Speech playback reads example words, not isolated phonemes, because device voices may pronounce isolated letters inconsistently.

Complete phonics learning pathway

Foundations

Letter names, letter sounds, phonemic awareness, rhyming and segmenting.

Decoding

Short vowels, CVC words, consonant blends, digraphs, final blends.

Advanced phonics

Silent-e, vowel teams, r-controlled vowels, diphthongs, soft c/g, syllable types.

Fluency

Multisyllabic words, common irregular words, punctuation, connected reading and comprehension.

English has more phonemes than letters, and phoneme inventories vary by dialect. This course groups spelling patterns for practical reading instruction rather than claiming one-to-one letter/sound correspondences. Device speech voices and pronunciation may vary.
